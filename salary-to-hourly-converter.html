How It Works — One Annual Figure Behind Every Conversion

This converter treats the year as the pivot. Whatever period you enter, it first works out the annual gross pay, then derives every other period from that single annual figure — so all seven numbers always agree with each other. An hourly rate is multiplied by your hours per week and your paid weeks per year; a weekly amount is multiplied by paid weeks; a biweekly amount is multiplied by 26; a semi-monthly amount by 24; and a monthly amount by 12.

From the annual figure it then divides back down: weekly is annual divided by paid weeks, hourly is weekly divided by hours per week, daily is weekly divided by days per week, biweekly is annual divided by 26, semi-monthly is annual divided by 24, and monthly is annual divided by 12. Change the hours, days or paid weeks and every equivalent updates together.

Gross pay before tax: every figure on this page is gross pay — the full amount before income tax, Social Security, Medicare, benefits or any other withholding. That is deliberate: take-home pay depends on where you live, your filing status and your benefits, so for take-home pay use the tax calculators instead of guessing here. This converter also assumes a straight rate with no overtime premium.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between biweekly and semi-monthly pay?

Biweekly means every two weeks: 26 paydays a year, usually on the same weekday, with two months holding a third payday. Semi-monthly means twice a month: 24 paydays a year, always on the same two dates (for example the 1st and 15th). The same annual salary therefore gives a larger semi-monthly cheque, because it is divided by 24 instead of 26.

How many work hours are there in a year?

2,080 for a standard full-time schedule: 40 hours a week times 52 weeks. That is the figure behind most salary-to-hourly conversions. If you work 37.5 hours a week, the year holds 1,950 paid hours at 52 paid weeks; change the hours and paid weeks in the calculator and it uses your numbers instead.

Does this converter include tax or overtime?

No. Every figure here is gross pay before tax — before income tax, Social Security, Medicare, benefits or any other withholding — and it assumes a straight rate with no overtime premium. For take-home pay, use the tax calculators; for overtime, use the Overtime Calculator.

How do I convert an annual salary to an hourly rate?

Divide the annual salary by the hours you are paid for in a year: hours per week times paid weeks per year. At 40 hours a week and 52 paid weeks that is salary divided by 2,080 — so $60,000 a year is $60,000 / 2,080 = $28.85 per hour.

What happens if I take unpaid time off?

Choose 'Unpaid time off' in the calculator and enter the unpaid weeks. Paid weeks fall by that amount — for example 52 minus 2 unpaid weeks = 50 paid weeks — so the annual figure falls too, while the hourly rate for the weeks you do work stays the same.

Why is monthly pay not just 4 times weekly pay?

Because months are longer than 4 weeks. The correct conversion is weekly times 52 divided by 12. Using times 4 understates monthly gross by about 8%.

How it works — the formula

Start from whichever period you know. The converter turns it into annual gross pay first, then divides that annual figure into every other period. That single-pivot method is why the hourly, weekly and monthly figures on this page never contradict each other.

The formulas:
Annual = hourly × hours per week × paid weeks per year
Weekly = annual ÷ paid weeks  ·  Hourly = weekly ÷ hours per week  ·  Daily = weekly ÷ days per week
Biweekly = annual ÷ 26  ·  Semi-monthly = annual ÷ 24  ·  Monthly = annual ÷ 12

Worked examples

$25 per hour: at 40 hours a week and 52 paid weeks a year, annual pay is $25 × 40 × 52 = $52,000 per year. The same pay is $200.00 per day (5 days a week), $1,000.00 per week, $2,000.00 biweekly, $2,166.67 semi-monthly and $4,333.33 per month.

$60,000 per year: divide by the paid hours in a year — 40 × 52 = 2,080 hours — to get $60,000 ÷ 2,080 = $28.85 per hour. The same salary is $1,153.85 per week and $5,000.00 per month.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use this to compare a salary offer with an hourly job?

Yes — that is one of its best uses. Convert the salary to an hourly rate with the hours the job actually expects, and convert the hourly offer to an annual figure with the same hours and paid weeks, then compare like with like. Remember both figures are gross: taxes, benefits, paid time off and commuting costs can change which offer is really better.

What if I work part-time or irregular hours?

Enter your real average hours per week and paid weeks per year instead of 40 and 52. For example, 20 hours a week at $20 per hour for 52 paid weeks is $20,800 a year. The formulas do not change — only the inputs do.

Why does the biweekly figure use 26, even when paid weeks are not 52?

Because a biweekly schedule always has 26 paydays in a standard year, by definition: 52 weeks divided by 2. Semi-monthly always has 24 and monthly always has 12. Paid weeks only affect the hourly, daily and weekly derivations, which are the ones tied to the weeks you actually work.
