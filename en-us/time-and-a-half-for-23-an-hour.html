What $23 an Hour Looks Like With Overtime

At a regular rate of $23/hour, time and a half is $34.50/hour and double time is $46.00/hour. Federal law owes the time-and-a-half rate on every hour past 40 in a workweek (state daily rules in CA, AK, CO, NV and OR can owe it sooner). Full-time at $23 with no overtime is $920.00 a week, about $3,986.67 a month and $47,840.00 a year before taxes — the baseline every row below builds on.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is time and a half for $23 an hour?

$23 × 1.5 = $34.50 per hour. You earn that rate for every overtime hour — federally, every hour past 40 in a workweek.

How much is 5 hours of overtime at $23 an hour?

5 × $34.50 = $172.50 in overtime pay, on top of $920.00 regular pay for the first 40 hours — a 45-hour week grosses $1,092.50 before taxes.

What is double time for $23 an hour?

$23 × 2 = $46.00 per hour. Double time is not required by federal law; it applies under California's daily rules (past 12 hours in a day), some union contracts and many holiday policies.

Is a 50-hour week at $23 worth it?

It grosses $1,265.00 — $345.00 of that is overtime pay, and $115.00 of it is pure premium over straight time. Whether it is worth it depends on taxes and rest, but the premium part alone equals 5 extra straight-time hours.
