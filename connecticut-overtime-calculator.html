Connecticut Overtime Calculator — Your Week Under Connecticut Rules

How It Works — The Connecticut Rule

Connecticut follows the federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) rule and nothing stricter for most private employers: overtime at one and a half times your regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. The rules below are the ones this calculator applies:

  • Weekly overtime: every hour over 40 in a single workweek is paid at 1.5× your regular rate.
  • No daily overtime: Connecticut has no daily overtime law for most private-sector jobs — one long day is regular time unless the week passes 40 hours.
  • No averaging: the workweek stands alone. A busy week cannot be balanced against a quiet week on the next paycheck.

Connecticut minimum wage (2026): $16.94/hour. Source for overtime rules: https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/overtime. Source for the minimum wage: US Department of Labor, State Minimum Wage Laws table. Last verified: November 2026.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does overtime work in Connecticut?

Connecticut follows the federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) rule and nothing stricter for most private employers: overtime at one and a half times your regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. Premium hours are paid at 1.5× your regular rate, and an hour is never paid at two premiums at once.

What is the minimum wage in Connecticut in 2026?

The 2026 Connecticut minimum wage is $16.94 per hour for most workers. Some cities set higher local rates, and tipped and youth rates can differ.

How much is 45 hours at the Connecticut minimum wage?

Five 9-hour days at $16.94: 40.00 regular hours ($677.60) plus 5.00 overtime hours at $25.41 ($127.05) = $804.65 gross before taxes.

Is there seventh-day premium pay in Connecticut?

In Connecticut restaurants and hotel restaurants, the seventh consecutive day of work in a week earns premium pay at 1.5× the minimum wage rate under state law — a sector rule that sits on top of the federal 40-hour overtime this calculator applies.

Can my employer average my hours over a two-week pay period?

No. Overtime is calculated per workweek, not per pay period. A 50-hour week followed by a 30-hour week is still 10 hours of overtime in week one, even when one paycheck covers both weeks.

How it works — the formula

The formula: Score the week, not the day. Add up the paid hours in the workweek; the first 40 are regular and everything beyond is overtime. Gross = (regular hours × rate) + (overtime hours × rate × 1.5).

Frequently asked questions

Why didn't my 12-hour day produce any overtime?

Because the rule is weekly. Twelve hours in a day is regular time if the workweek ends at 40 hours or less; the premium starts only when the week's total passes 40. States with daily overtime are the exception, not the norm.

What changes if my break is paid?

Paid break minutes stay in your paid time instead of coming off the shift. That can move a day or a week across a threshold: a 9:00-to-6:30 shift with a paid 30-minute break holds 9.5 paid hours, not 9.
