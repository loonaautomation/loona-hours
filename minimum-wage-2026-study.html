Minimum Wage 2026 — All 51 US Jurisdictions

One citable page with the official 2026 minimum wage for every US state and the District of Columbia: the headline findings, a chart, the full table for all 51 jurisdictions, and a free CSV download of the same dataset.

In short: In 2026 the District of Columbia has the highest minimum wage in the United States at $18.40/hour; Washington is the highest state at $17.13/hour. 31 of the 51 jurisdictions set a rate above the federal minimum of $7.25, while 20 jurisdictions sit exactly at the federal rate. The median jurisdiction rate is $11.85/hour.

Key findings

  • Highest: District of Columbia — $18.40/hour. The highest state is Washington at $17.13/hour, followed by Connecticut ($16.94) and California ($16.90).
  • Lowest: $7.25/hour, the federal minimum wage. 20 jurisdictions — Alabama, Georgia, Idaho, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Mississippi, New Hampshire, North Carolina, North Dakota, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Wisconsin and Wyoming — sit at the federal rate.
  • Above federal: 31 of 51 jurisdictions (30 states + DC) set a 2026 rate above $7.25.
  • $15 club: 19 jurisdictions are at $15.00/hour or more; 30 are at $10.00/hour or more.
  • Middle of the pack: the median rate is $11.85/hour and the average is $11.53/hour — a spread of $11.15 between the highest and lowest jurisdiction.

Methodology and sources

Rates are the standard statewide (or district-wide) minimum wage for 2026 for each of the 50 states and the District of Columbia. Figures were verified against the U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) 2026 minimum wage figures on 7 November 2026, and each rate on this page matches the rate published on that jurisdiction’s own Loona Hours minimum wage page. City and local rates are not included in this dataset; they are covered on the Minimum Wage 2026 hub page. Rates are refreshed every January and July, and after any DOL update.
