California Overtime Rules, Explained — Daily Overtime, Double Time & the 7th Day

California prices overtime by the day, not the week. Here is the full rule set, the exceptions, and how to check a stub against it.

In short: In California, hours 9–12 of a workday pay 1.5×, hours past 12 pay 2×, and the seventh consecutive day pays premiums from the first hour — with the weekly 40-hour rule underneath and no hour ever paid twice.

Why California is different

Most of America prices overtime by the week: nothing special happens until hour 41. California prices it by the day. The rules come from Labor Code section 510 and the Industrial Welfare Commission wage orders, and they make California the most employee-protective overtime state in the country — as well as the easiest state in which to misread a pay stub.

Three layers apply at once: daily overtime, double time, and the seventh-day rule, with the federal-style weekly rule running underneath as a safety net. An hour is only ever paid at one premium — the system takes the most generous applicable treatment — which accountants call the ban on “pyramiding.”

The daily rules, hour by hour

  • Hours 1–8 in a workday: your regular rate.
  • Hours 9–12: time and a half (1.5×).
  • Hours beyond 12: double time (2×).
  • Seventh consecutive day in a workweek: the first 8 hours are paid at 1.5× and anything beyond at 2× — even if the day is short.
  • Weekly rule underneath: hours over 40 in the workweek are 1.5×, counting only hours not already paid at a daily premium.

A workday in California is any consecutive 24-hour period beginning at the same time each day, as set by the employer; a workweek is seven workdays. Because the daily split happens first, a California employee can earn overtime in a 30-hour week (one brutal 14-hour day contains 4 hours at 1.5× and 2 at 2×) — something impossible under the federal rule.

Worked example — a mixed week at $20/hour

Monday to Friday, 9:00 AM to 6:30 PM with a 30-minute unpaid meal break: 9 paid hours a day, splitting 8 regular + 1 overtime daily (40 regular, 5 overtime). Saturday, 9:00 AM to 10:30 PM: 13 paid hours — 8 regular, 4 overtime, 1 double time. Sunday, the seventh consecutive day, 9:00 AM to 7:30 PM: 10 hours — 8 overtime, 2 double time. Week total: 68 hours = 40 regular ($800) + 25 overtime ($750) + 3 double time ($120) = $1,670. Alternative workweeks: the 4/10 exception

California allows employers to adopt an alternative workweek schedule — classically four 10-hour days — through a formal process: a written proposal, a secret-ballot election passed by two-thirds of the affected work unit, and filing with the state. Under a valid 4/10, the daily trigger moves to 10 hours: hours 11–12 are 1.5× and beyond 12 is 2×. Hours on a fifth day are overtime.

The formality matters. A manager simply announcing “we work 4/10s now” does not create an alternative workweek; without a valid election, hour 9 and 10 of every day remain daily overtime. Health-care employers have their own 12-hour variant under Wage Order 5, which is why hospital schedules need individual checking.

Meal and rest break premiums — the part calculators leave out

California separately polices breaks: a 30-minute unpaid meal period before the end of the fifth hour (a second one on shifts over 10 hours, waivable in narrow cases), and a paid 10-minute rest break for roughly every 4 hours worked. When a compliant break is not provided, the penalty is one extra hour of pay at the employee's regular rate per violation per day — up to two hours per day (one for meal, one for rest).

That premium depends on whether breaks were actually offered and taken, which no hours-only calculator can know. It is deliberately excluded from our estimates — but if your shifts regularly run past five hours with no meal break on the stub, you may be owed an extra hour a day, and that claim is worth raising with specifics.

Reporting-time pay and split shifts: two more California premiums

Two California premiums surprise even experienced workers. Reporting-time pay: if you report for your scheduled shift and are sent home with less than half the usual hours — a cancelled event shift, a slow restaurant night — the employer owes half of your usual or scheduled day's hours at your regular rate, not less than 2 hours and not more than 4. Being called in on a day off and sent home within two hours triggers the 2-hour minimum on its own.

Split-shift premium: when your day is split by an unpaid break longer than a meal period — the classic 10 AM–2 PM and 5 PM–9 PM restaurant day — California owes one extra hour at the state minimum wage. If you earn more than minimum wage, the excess you earn over the minimum across the day is credited against that hour, so the premium shrinks as your wage rises and disappears for many higher-paid staff.

Neither premium is overtime, and neither appears in an hours calculator — but both belong on a correct California stub, and both are worth checking on any week your schedule was chopped up.

Who is exempt in California

California's exemptions are stricter than the federal ones. The executive, administrative and professional exemptions require both a duties test and a salary of at least twice the state minimum wage for full-time work — $70,304 a year in 2026 at the $16.90 minimum wage (2 × $16.90 × 2,080 hours). Computer professionals have their own hourly/salary thresholds, adjusted annually. Outside salespeople and certain other categories follow special rules.

Misclassification is the most expensive error in California employment law precisely because daily overtime and break premiums stack: two years of mispriced 10-hour days is a large number, which is why this state produces so much wage litigation.

Checking a California pay stub

  • Each day should split visibly at 8 and 12 hours; a week total alone cannot prove a California stub right.
  • Seventh-day hours should carry premiums from the first hour — look at any week you worked all seven days.
  • Break premiums appear (when owed) as a separate line, often labelled “meal premium” or similar.
  • Itemized statements are your legal right in California (Labor Code 226) — the stub must show hours, rates and gross/net.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is double time after 12 hours or after 8 on the seventh day?

Both: hours beyond 12 in any workday are double time, and on the seventh consecutive day of a workweek, hours beyond the first 8 are double time.

Can I waive California overtime?

No. Agreements to waive statutory overtime are not enforceable — the premium is owed even if you signed something, and “comp time” cannot replace it at private employers.

Does California overtime apply to salaried employees?

Only to non-exempt ones. Exempt status in California needs a real duties test plus a salary of at least twice the state minimum wage for full-time work ($70,304/year in 2026).

What is the California minimum wage in 2026?

$16.90 per hour statewide (some cities are higher). It adjusts each January 1.

Are meal break penalties part of overtime?

No — they are a separate premium of one hour's pay per violation per day, and they are not included in calculator estimates because they depend on break practices, not just hours.
