How It Works — The Rule Behind This Time Card

Wyoming follows the federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) rule and nothing stricter for most private employers: overtime at one and a half times your regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. The card first turns each shift into paid minutes (clock-out minus clock-in, minus unpaid breaks, with overnight shifts rolled into the next day), optionally rounds punches the way payroll does, and then splits the week under Wyoming's rule:

  • Weekly overtime: every hour over 40 in a single workweek is paid at 1.5× your regular rate.
  • No daily overtime: Wyoming has no daily overtime law for most private-sector jobs — one long day is regular time unless the week passes 40 hours.
  • No averaging: the workweek stands alone. A busy week cannot be balanced against a quiet week on the next paycheck.

Wyoming minimum wage (2026): $7.25/hour. Wyoming's own state rate is $5.15, but employers covered by the federal FLSA must pay $7.25 — the rate that applies to most Wyoming workers. How it works — the formula

The formula: Paid minutes per day = (clock-out − clock-in) − unpaid break. Then: Score the week, not the day. Add up the paid hours in the workweek; the first 40 are regular and everything beyond is overtime. Gross = (regular hours × rate) + (overtime hours × rate × 1.5).

Worked example

Monday to Saturday, 9:00 AM to 5:30 PM with a 30-minute unpaid break — 8 paid hours a day, 48 for the week — at the Wyoming minimum wage of $7.25/hour: the card totals 48:00 hours — 40:00 regular and 8:00 overtime, for a gross of $377.00 ($290.00 + $87.00). That is the week already loaded in the calculator above.

Frequently asked questions

Why didn't my 12-hour day produce any overtime?

Because the rule is weekly. Twelve hours in a day is regular time if the workweek ends at 40 hours or less; the premium starts only when the week's total passes 40. States with daily overtime are the exception, not the norm.

What changes if my break is paid?

Paid break minutes stay in your paid time instead of coming off the shift. That can move a day or a week across a threshold: a 9:00-to-6:30 shift with a paid 30-minute break holds 9.5 paid hours, not 9.
